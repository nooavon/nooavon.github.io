## Question

A (fictional) pension institution wants to invest in a sustainable fund. Which fund in the Finnish mutual fund market should it choose, and does "sustainable" actually mean better returns or a greener portfolio?

## Approach

- Built a dataset of 58 funds sold in Finland, 14 of them labelled sustainable, with fund size, 2025 capital flows, returns over 1 month to 10 years, Sharpe ratios, SFDR category and ESG Quality Score.
- Compared sustainable funds with the rest of the market on returns, risk-adjusted returns and ESG scores.
- Compared the results with van der Beck (2025), who argues that ESG funds' excess returns come from investor inflows, not from better fundamentals.
- Scored each sustainable fund's marketing material against a five-part rubric: clarity of the sustainability claim, binding commitments, risk disclosure, return reporting, and accountability.

**My part:** I cleaned the fund data and did part of the analysis. I made the market share and capital flow slides (slides 4–5) and contributed to some of the other slides with the group.

## Key findings

- **Sustainable funds are a fifth of the market.** They hold €7.9bn of the €40.4bn sample, and a single Nordea fund accounts for €6.2bn of that.
- **Money flowed out in 2025.** The largest sustainable fund shrank by €2.0bn during the year, which is 44% of all the money moving in or out of the funds in the sample.
- **No lasting return advantage.** Over 10 years, sustainable and other funds returned within 0.2 percentage points of each other per year, and sustainable funds had lower risk-adjusted returns. This fits van der Beck's view that ESG outperformance comes from inflows and fades when they stop.
- **"Sustainable" is only slightly greener.** Sustainable funds score only 0.22 points higher on ESG on a size-weighted basis (7.22 vs 7.00), and 6 of 31 conventional funds beat the sustainable median.
- **Recommendation: OP Climate A.** It returned 16.2% a year over five years (4.3 percentage points above the sustainable-fund average), is large enough for a pension investor, had net inflows in a year of outflows, and scored highest on our marketing-transparency rubric.
